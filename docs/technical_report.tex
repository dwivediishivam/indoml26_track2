\title{Event-Gated Ensemble Enhancement with ASR-Consistent Training for Indic Noise Event Removal}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Shivam Dwivedi, Animish Yadav, Shubhi Jain}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Team TDS, Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology Delhi (IIIT Delhi), India\\
\{shivam21352, animish23089, shubhi23517\}@iiitd.ac.in}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present our Track~2 system for the Datathon@IndoML~2026 noise event removal challenge: suppressing non-speech
events (horns, animals, breathing, children, alarms, machinery, music) in real-world Indic speech from the Vaani
corpus, scored by SI-SDR on synthetic clips plus $100\times$ the WER reduction of the frozen mandated ASR
(SraVaani-1.0) over all clips. A single learned enhancer is applied identically to every clip. It averages a
waveform-domain DNS64 (Demucs) model and a band-split RNN with a learned frame-level \emph{event gate}, both trained
on on-the-fly mixtures whose event statistics follow the validation set and with an \emph{ASR-consistency loss}
through the frozen SraVaani encoder. A second stage fine-tunes both models on longer, denser and louder mixtures,
with checkpoints selected on a \emph{pseudo-test} built from held-out validation clips that predicted our
leaderboard score within 0.13. Our final submission (Codabench 995085) reaches SI-SDR 12.78\,dB and $\Delta$WER
$+1.87$ (synthetic $+5.41$), a combined score of 14.65 (Phase~2 rank~13), with the hidden ASR audit passed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
speech enhancement, noise event removal, band-split RNN, Demucs, ASR-aware training, Indic speech
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Training Data and Preprocessing}
\subsection{Data sources}
Table~\ref{tab:data} lists every source. From the \textbf{Vaani Noise Event Dataset}~\cite{vaani} (90,637 segments,
154.6\,h, Gold/Silver/Bronze tiers) we keep only clean speech: non-event regions of at least 1.5\,s, at least
0.25\,s from any labelled event, from clips whose 10th-percentile frame energy lies more than 28\,dB below the clip
RMS. Of the \textbf{IndoML validation set} (2,501 clips; 1,806 natural, 695 synthetic with clean references), the
695 synthetic pairs are split 560/135 into training and holdout. Noise events are cut from
$\text{noisy}-\text{clean}$ at the labelled spans ($\pm$50\,ms) of the 560 training pairs; in these pairs the added
noise lies entirely inside the spans. The external \textbf{ESC-50}~\cite{esc50} clips (resampled to 16\,kHz,
trimmed to the region within 30\,dB of the peak) make the model generalise beyond the validation noise, and Indic
\textbf{FLEURS}~\cite{fleurs} shards add clean read speech. All banks are stored as memory-mapped float16 arrays.

\subsection{Augmentation pipeline}
Mixtures are generated on the fly, so every batch is new:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Speech.} A validation reference ($p{=}0.5$) or a Vaani/FLEURS segment. Utterances rescaled within
$\pm$3\,dB and separated by 50--600\,ms gaps are joined until the 6--10\,s crop is full; the crop is scaled to
$-30\ldots-15$\,dBFS.
\item \emph{Event count} $k=n\cdot U(1,L/4.5\,\text{s})\cdot U(\text{ev\_mult})$, with $n$ drawn from the per-clip
counts of the validation synthetic set.
\item \emph{Events.} A validation noise event ($p{=}0.7$) or an ESC-50 crop of empirical duration ($p{=}0.3$),
with 5\,ms fades and random polarity at a uniform offset. Its level relative to the speech RMS follows the
empirical validation distribution (median $+4.3$\,dB) $+\,\mathcal{N}(0,2)$\,dB $+\,U(\text{db\_shift})$.
\item \emph{Targets.} Clean speech and the frame event mask (hop 128); mixture and target are peak-normalised jointly.
\end{enumerate}
The simulated mixtures match validation (median input SI-SDR $-2.5$ vs.\ $-2.6$\,dB; event coverage 43\% vs.\
45\%). Stage~2 uses $\text{ev\_mult}{=}(1,2)$, $\text{db\_shift}{=}(0,3)$\,dB and 10\,s crops.

\section{Proposed Method}
\subsection{Architecture}
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the system.

\textbf{DNS64.} The Demucs waveform U-Net with an LSTM bottleneck~\cite{demucs} (hidden width 64, 33.5\,M
parameters), initialised from the public checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:pre}).

\textbf{Event-gated BSRNN} (ours, 11.3\,M parameters), after band-split RNN~\cite{bsrnn}. A 512-point STFT (hop
128) is split into 37 bands ($16{\times}4$, $16{\times}8$, $4{\times}16$ and 1 bins), each normalised and projected
to 112 features. Six blocks alternate residual BLSTMs over time and over bands, and per-band GLU MLPs output a
complex mask $\mathbf{M}$, giving $\hat{s}=\text{iSTFT}(\mathbf{M}\odot\mathbf{X})$. A gate head maps the band
features to an event probability $p(t)$ and the output is
\begin{equation}
y = x + g(t)\,(\hat{s}-x),\qquad g(t)=\gamma+(1-\gamma)\,p(t),
\end{equation}
with floor $\gamma=0.2$, so every frame is processed while event-free speech stays close to the input.

\subsection{Losses}
For mixture $x$, clean target $s$ and event mask $m$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{BSRNN}} ={}& \tfrac12\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(\hat{s},s)+\tfrac12\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(y,s)
+ \text{BCE}(p,m) + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ASR}},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(a,s) ={}& -\text{SI-SDR}(a,s) + 0.5\,\text{MRSTFT}(a,s),
\end{align}
and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DNS64}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}(\hat{s},s)+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{ASR}}$. SI-SDR is the
metric itself~\cite{sisdr}; MRSTFT is the L1 distance between log-magnitudes at FFT sizes 256, 512 and
1024~\cite{pwg}. The \emph{ASR-consistency loss} $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ASR}}$ is the L1 distance between the frozen
SraVaani encoder outputs on the enhanced and clean signals, normalised by the target's mean magnitude, applied every
second step with $\lambda=0.1$. Its front end replicates the ASR's pre-emphasis, 128-bin log-mel and per-feature
normalisation, and gradients flow through the frozen encoder, so the enhancer learns outputs that the mandated ASR
transcribes like clean speech.

\subsection{Training strategy}
All runs use AdamW~\cite{adamw} (weight decay $10^{-2}$), a 500-step warm-up and a cosine schedule driven by
\emph{wall-clock time}, which guarantees completion inside a fixed GPU allocation, with gradient clipping at 5 and
bf16 autocast for BSRNN. Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the runs.

\textbf{Pseudo-test.} After our first submission revealed a large gap between validation and test, we built a
pseudo-test only from the 135 holdout clips: 106 clips of 2--3 joined holdout references (median 13.5\,s) carrying
their own noise plus $0.5\times$ extra holdout events at $+1.5$\,dB. Its median input SI-SDR ($-4.5$\,dB) matches the
level we estimated for the real synthetic test clips ($-4.48$\,dB), and it reproduces the per-clip score
distribution of our first submission (median 12.79 vs.\ 12.27\,dB; 5th percentile 4.78 vs.\ 4.59\,dB).
\textbf{Stage~2} fine-tunes DNS64 and BSRNN on these test-like conditions (BSRNN's clean pool excludes Vaani
segments).

\textbf{Inference.} The final system averages the two stage-2 models (gate floor 0.2), matches the output RMS to
the input, processes 20\,s chunks with a 2\,s cross-fade and writes PCM-16. Transcripts come from the unmodified
SraVaani-1.0 (fp32, greedy TDT~\cite{tdt}) on the written WAVs.

\textbf{Candidate selection.} Candidates are ranked by an \emph{estimated leaderboard score}: pseudo-test SI-SDR
minus a 0.70\,dB calibration offset, plus $0.32\times$ the synthetic $\Delta$WER (holdout clips, against
ASR-on-clean transcripts, calibrated to our first submission), plus $0.68\times$ the natural-validation $\Delta$WER;
0.32 and 0.68 are the synthetic and natural shares of words in the test set.

Table~\ref{tab:res} and Figs.~\ref{fig:curves}--\ref{fig:gate} summarise the results.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Final submission (995085):} SI-SDR 12.78\,dB and $\Delta$WER $+1.87$ ($+5.41$ on synthetic, $+0.21$
on natural clips), combined 14.65; the hidden ASR audit passed with 0\% mismatch.
\item \textbf{Architectural diversity helps.} DNS64 + BSRNN gains 0.75\,dB over either model alone and turns the
holdout $\Delta$WER positive, whereas averaging two DNS64 models adds nothing.
\item \textbf{The learned gate needs clean training speech.} The final BSRNN gated at $\gamma=0.2$ reaches
19.04\,dB against 17.82\,dB ungated; with a noisier clean pool the gate lowers SI-SDR instead
(Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a), consistent with activity targets that mislabel real background noise as speech.
\item \textbf{Noise level, not duration, limits performance.} On the scored test clips, per-clip SI-SDR correlates
0.82 with the estimated input SNR and 0.09 with clip length. Clips below $-6$\,dB input SNR (39\%) average
8.5\,dB, and clips above 0\,dB average 20.7\,dB. On the pseudo-test our ensemble (13.36\,dB) already exceeds the
ideal ratio mask (12.99\,dB) and approaches the ideal phase-sensitive mask (15.15\,dB), so mask-style enhancement
has little headroom left at such SNRs.
\item \textbf{Pseudo-test fidelity.} The estimator predicted 14.52 for our final entry, which scored 14.65, whereas
validation-holdout estimates were optimistic by about 5\,dB because test clips are 2.7$\times$ longer and much
noisier.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Novelty.} (i) An event-gated BSRNN that learns event activity jointly with separation; (ii)
ASR-consistency training through the frozen mandated ASR; (iii) wall-clock-scheduled training with
validation-matched on-the-fly mixing; and (iv) a holdout-derived pseudo-test with a calibrated estimate of the
leaderboard score, used to choose submissions.

\textbf{Conclusion.} A single learned, event-aware enhancer trained on statistically matched mixtures gives strong
SI-SDR and a positive ASR gain on natural and synthetic speech; very low-SNR clips remain the main gap.
